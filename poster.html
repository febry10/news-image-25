First Foreign Astronaut Flies to China’s Space Station

Shenzhou-24 roared off the pad at dawn carrying Pakistan’s Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, 40 — the first foreigner ever to visit the Tiangong station. Commander Tang Hongbo, on a record third trip to Tiangong, and flight engineer Wu Yi complete the crew. Ali, a payload specialist, will run experiments for about a week before returning on Shenzhou-23.

Huawei Convicted in the US: Guilty on 10 of 12 Counts

A federal jury convicted China’s Huawei of bank fraud, sanctions violations and stealing American trade secrets — Cisco source code, Quintel antenna designs, a CNEX circuit board — after a two-month trial with more than 60 witnesses. Jurors acquitted it of racketeering. The case opened in 2018 — the sharpest legal blow yet in the US–China technology war.

Drone Hits Riyadh’s Tallest Tower: 11 Injured

A Houthi drone slammed into the 80-storey PIF Tower in Riyadh’s financial district — the second strike on the tower in under a week. Eleven people were hurt: two Saudis, two Indians, three Nepalese and four Egyptians. The Saudi-led coalition vowed a “decisive” response and struck back in Yemen.

Microsoft Declares ‘Code Red’ After Worst-Ever Outage

A Code Red overhaul follows the 12–13 November outage, which left more customers without email than any incident in Exchange Online history, hitting governments from Australia to the US. The cause: a hidden Windows defect dating to July. CEO Satya Nadella has apologised; the new goal is a worldwide fix within one hour.

US Readies New Tariffs on 16 Trading Partners

Washington is set to unveil Section 301 duties after a probe into “excess industrial capacity” in China, the EU, India, Japan and 12 other partners. The levies would stack on existing tariffs and partly replace those struck down by the Supreme Court in February; rates of 15–25% are in play.
